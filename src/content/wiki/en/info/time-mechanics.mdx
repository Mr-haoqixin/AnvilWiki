## Difference From Traditional Life Simulators

*Songs of Glimmerwick* makes a key decision on time mechanics: **unlike other life simulators, the clock freezes when an event starts or night falls**. In games like *Stardew Valley*, time keeps flowing, and players are often pushed by "what time is it, where should I go" anxiety; this game does the opposite, stopping the clock at key moments.

## Infinite Exploration Time

The direct effect of the clock freeze is **giving players unlimited time to explore and complete tasks**. When night falls or an event starts, you don't have to rush through a location in a limited time — you can search slowly, try melodies slowly, and chat with NPCs slowly; time won't slip away while you explore.

## Removing the Urgency

This design **removes the urgency of life simulation games**. Players don't need to check schedules and optimize routes to "not waste a day". For players who prefer slow-paced exploration and atmospheric immersion, this is a relief; for players who enjoy time-management pressure, it may remove a layer of challenge fun.

## Actively Pausing the Clock

When **an important class or scheduled feature is about to start**, the player can actively pause the clock. This means even if you know there's a class coming up, you can finish what's in front of you first, without worrying about being late the moment you turn around.

## Design Intent

Overall, the time mechanics reflect this game's orientation: **letting players explore the world at ease**, rather than racing against time. This is consistent with the game's positioning of "replacing combat with music, exploration, gardening, and socializing" — slow, generous, and atmosphere-focused is the experience it aims to give.
