## Core Innovation: Melody Is Spell

The most identifiable mechanic of *Songs of Glimmerwick* is making spellcasting and musical performance one thing: the player plays notes to form melodies, every song carries magic, and melody is spell. Spellcasting is no longer pressing a hotkey to release a skill, but personally playing a tune.

## Melodies and Magic Effects

Different melodies correspond to different magic effects; known typical uses include:

- **Talking to trees**: Use a specific melody to awaken trees and converse with them.
- **Summoning clouds for rain**: Play a rainfall melody to change local weather, serving gardening.
- **Enchanting tools**: Use melodies to add magic effects to tools, boosting their abilities.

## Proficiency and Class Progress

Magic growth is directly tied to class progress. As proficiency increases, players can unlock more complex melody combinations, stacking multiple effects. For example, a rainfall melody and a growth-acceleration melody can be played in sequence, first summoning rain then boosting growth, completing two things in one spellcast. Better playing enhances spell effects, so "practicing songs well" is itself a way to get stronger.

## Flute and Rhythm Sequences

The **Flute** is the player's main magic instrument. Learning a new song requires completing a rhythm sequence — think of it as "playing out" the melody following prompts. Once a song is mastered, it enters the player's available spell library, ready to be played for spellcasting at any time.

## Scale of Learnable Spells

According to information released by the developer, there are over **300 million spells** learnable in the game, of which over **20,000** are for changing the weather. This scale means players can almost never "finish learning", and exploring and collecting melodies is itself a long-term goal.
